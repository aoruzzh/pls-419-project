## What the state measures, and what the numbers show

The DFC applies the Methodology for Determining the Social Well-being of a Family (Person), Ministry of Labour Order No. 267 of 29 June 2023, developed with UNDP (Zakon.uchet.kz, 2023; Ministry of Labour, 2023). UNDP and ITU describe it as based on the Alkire–Foster multidimensional poverty method (ITU, n.d.). Press reports describe 59 indicators across eight areas: income and loans, transport, employment, social protection, health, education, housing and agriculture (Azattyq Rýhy, 2026b; Informburo.kz, 2026a). Points are added or subtracted for each, and the total sets the category.

Point values come from before the 29 October 2026 revision and may have changed. One outlet reported seven categories rather than five (Azattyq Rýhy, 2026a); every other source says five.

## Figure 1 · One tenge can change a family's category

Figure 1. Score of a family of three (two earners, one child) at different per-person incomes, using the points reported above. Own calculation. Archetype, not a real family. Source of rules: Finratings (2026), Azattyq Rýhy (2026a), Digital Business (2026). Subsistence minimum: Kapital.kz (2025).

The rule works in steps. For this family, every income band below four subsistence minimums leaves them in category C. Cross 203,404 tenge per person and their income points jump from 400 to 620, the score rises from 650 to 870, and the family moves into B. In 2026 that would have cost it the birth benefit of 164,350 tenge for a new child (NUR.kz, 2026). Economists call this a notch: a small gain in income produces a large loss in support. The rule also counts income per person without any adjustment for household size, which treats a family of six like six single people.

## Figure 2 · Benefits are losing value to inflation

Figure 2. One-time birth benefit for a first to third child, nominal and in December-2020 prices. Own calculation. Benefit = 38 × MRP (MRP: CDB.kz, n.d.; Zakon.kz, 2025). Prices: December-on-December CPI, Bureau of National Statistics (2024, 2026).

Most of these benefits are set in monthly calculation indices (MRP). The birth benefit is 38 MRP (NUR.kz, 2026). Between January 2021 and January 2026 the MRP rose 48%, from 2,917 to 4,325 tenge. Over the same five years consumer prices rose about 75%: 8.4% in 2021, 20.3% in 2022, 9.8% in 2023, 8.6% in 2024 and 12.3% in 2025 (Bureau of National Statistics, 2024, 2026). In real terms the benefit fell from about 110,800 to 94,100 tenge in 2020 prices, a loss of about 15%. So while the reform debates who should get support, the support itself has been shrinking for those who keep it.

Method note: January benefit values are deflated by the December-on-December CPI of the previous year. The mid-2022 MRP increase for benefits (3,180 ₸ from April) is not shown; including it would not change the trend.

## Where the data is silent

- The rule itself. Sources disagree on basic facts: 30 or more than 120 source databases (Kapital.kz, 2026; ITU, 2025); 59, 80 or more than 100 indicators (Azattyq Rýhy, 2026b; UNDP Kazakhstan, n.d.; ITU, n.d.); 10, 14 or 39 benefits; five or seven categories. A citizen cannot easily find the full, current points table.
- Informal income. Families paid in cash, or working abroad, look poorer or richer than they are depending on what the registries record.
- Data errors. No public figures exist on how many DFC records are wrong, how many families have asked for a correction, or how many appeals succeeded.
- Error rates. The government has published the savings it expects, but not an estimate of how many needy families might be scored A or B. That figure needs survey microdata (the Household Budget Survey) linked to DFC categories, which is not public.
- Bank data. From 2027, banks will pass account data on benefit applicants to the state with their consent (Sputnik Kazakhstan, 2026). How that data will be weighted is not published.

## How far can the numbers be trusted?

Price and MRP data are official and reliable. The scoring rule is reliable in outline (five categories, the A/B cut-off) but uncertain in detail, because the points circulating in the press predate the October 2026 revision. Savings figures are political estimates, not costed projections. Error rates are unknown. That is the gap this project addresses.
