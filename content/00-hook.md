Families across Kazakhstan began receiving notices: from 1 January, some of the benefits they count on (the payment when a baby is born, the monthly childcare allowance) would depend on a letter of the alphabet. A, B, C, D or E. The letter is not chosen by a social worker. It is calculated by the Digital Family Card, a government system that reads more than six million families' records from state databases and turns them into a score.

A family in category A or B loses the listed benefits. A family that earns one tenge more than a line on a table can jump from C to B. A family whose old car is still registered, or whose income is paid in cash, may be scored as richer or poorer than it really is.

This site asks a simple question with a complicated answer: who does the algorithm miss, and can AI help Kazakhstan see them?
